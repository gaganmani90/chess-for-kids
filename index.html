What We'll Learn 📚

A peek for parents at the chess journey ahead. Each session builds on the last — your child moves from naming the pieces to capturing like a champ. 👑

Lesson 1

Meet & Count the Pieces

Goal: Recognize every piece and count them confidently.

  • Each side has 8 pawns, 2 rooks, 2 bishops, 2 knights, 1 queen, 1 king.
  • 16 pieces per player, 32 in total, on a board of 64 squares.
  • Your child can answer "how many?" questions about any piece.
Lesson 2

How the Bishop & Rook Move

Goal: Learn the first two sliding pieces.

  • The Rook moves in straight lines — up, down, left, right.
  • The Bishop moves diagonally.
  • Neither piece can jump over others.
Lesson 3

Queen, King & Pawn Moves

Goal: Round out the basic moves.

  • The Queen combines rook and bishop — any straight or diagonal line.
  • The King moves one square in any direction.
  • The Pawn moves forward one square (two on its first move).
Lesson 4

Practical Session 1 — Sliding Pieces

Goal: Move pieces on a real board.

  • Hands-on practice moving the Bishop, Rook, and Queen.
  • Reinforces straight vs. diagonal paths.
  • Builds comfort placing and sliding pieces correctly.
Lesson 5

Practical Session 2 — King & Pawn

Goal: Practice the short-range pieces.

  • Hands-on practice moving the King and the Pawn.
  • Reinforces the King's one-square range.
  • Reinforces the Pawn's forward-only rule.
Lesson 6

The Knight

Goal: Master the trickiest piece.

  • The Knight moves in an "L" shape.
  • It's the only piece that can jump over others.
  • Your child learns to spot all of its landing squares.
Lesson 7

Moving Multiple Pieces

Goal: Coordinate several pieces together.

  • Combines every move learned so far.
  • Practice planning with more than one piece on the board.
  • First taste of thinking a move ahead.
Lesson 8

How to Capture

Goal: Understand what a capture is and why it matters.

  • A capture means landing on an enemy piece's square to remove it.
  • Each piece captures along the same path it moves.
  • Capturing wins material — a key step toward winning the game.
